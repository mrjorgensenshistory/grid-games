What you are doing

One semester. Roughly a hundred years. On this board it all becomes one question: what caused what?

You will lay down a spine of ten big events in order, hang the smaller pieces off it, and draw an arrow every time you believe one thing caused another. Every arrow you draw, you have to defend in a sentence.

  • An arrow is a claim. It points from the cause to the effect. Pointing it the wrong way is a different claim, and a wrong one.
  • Some pieces in the tray do not belong in the chain at all. They happened in these years. That is not the same as causing anything. Leave them in the tray if that is what you believe.
  • Some causes come back around. If you can find a circle, where an effect turns into a cause of the thing that made it, you have found the most interesting thing on this board.

You are graded on your sentences, not on matching an answer key. An unusual arrow you defend well beats an obvious arrow you say nothing about.

Loops

A loop is a circle of arrows: A caused B, B caused C, and C came back around to cause more A. Loops are why some problems kept getting worse instead of settling down.

If your board has one, it is listed below. Name it and you get credit for finding it. If nothing is listed, go back to the board and look for a circle.
